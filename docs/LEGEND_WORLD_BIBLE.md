# LEGEND — World & Story Bible

**Document status:** Working canon reference, version 1.1 — fantasy RPG direction reconciliation in progress  
**Scope:** The shared world of LEGEND, beginning with *LEGEND: The Unquiet Road* (recommended working subtitle)  
**Purpose:** Give future games, stories, art, audio, and adaptations one consistent foundation.

This is an internal production document, not an in-world encyclopedia. It records what the current game and design documents establish, distinguishes open mysteries from facts, and gives future additions a continuity check.

## CURRENT DIRECTION OVERRIDE — MONSTER WARS & THE AWAKENING

**This is the current preferred creative direction.** Use [Fantasy RPG Direction: Monster Wars & the Awakening](LEGEND_FANTASY_RPG_DIRECTION.md) as the narrative north star and [Game Reboot & Playable Foundation Plan](LEGEND_GAME_REBOOT_PLAN.md) for the current candidate Veil / Five Arts / Last Mercy / Afterwake system and gameplay direction. LEGEND is a dark fantasy RPG about a spreading monster incursion, the return of old magic, regional conflicts, and a deeper power that may be awakening. The world must offer tangible adventure—monsters, dungeons, factions, exploration, crafting, training, and meaningful regional stories—before it asks the player to solve a supernatural mystery.

This direction supersedes the earlier road/map-centered mythology as the primary franchise explanation. Bells, lanterns, route markers, cairns, ledgers, and the Old Road remain established motifs and useful story objects, but they do **not** explain every mystery or every monster. The Pale Surveyor, Hollow Mile, Unreturned, Four Witnesses, Office of Measure, Seven-Wagon Dispatch, Aren Vey, and Ivara are legacy proposals, not approved central canon. They may be retained as optional or secondary lore only after review.

**Reconciliation rule:** preserve implemented facts and character identities; reframe clues only where their observed evidence supports it; demote or retire repetitive abstractions; keep the origin of the awakening and its relationship to the invasion open until the arcs have been reconciled. This is a narrative documentation change; it does not by itself change game code or imply that planned regions/systems are implemented.


## 1. The franchise promise

LEGEND is grounded dark fantasy RPG about a frontier world under pressure from monster incursions while old magic returns. It combines practical adventure—dangerous expeditions, creatures, ruins, factions, training, crafting, and regional conflicts—with a slowly unfolding mystery about what is driving the upheaval. Roads and journeys remain part of the world, but are not its sole identity or central cosmology.

**Player promise:** Prepare in a place that feels human, leave it for somewhere uncertain, make choices that change the journey, and return with more than loot: bring back evidence, relationships, and a different understanding of the world.

A story in LEGEND should do at least two of these things:
- Reveal something through a place, object, custom, or encounter rather than a lore lecture.
- Make a choice carry a consequence beyond the next line of dialogue.
- Show how people make a living in a difficult landscape.
- Let a strange event remain unsettling without immediately explaining it.
- Turn a practical object into a story-bearing object: a bell shard, lantern, map, route marker, ledger, or keepsake.

## 2. Tone and themes

### Core tone
Weathered, intimate, atmospheric dark fantasy. Rain-dark timber, old stone, mud, peat, iron, fog, candlelight, tarnished brass, parchment, and occasional cold-blue light. Danger is real, but the setting is not relentlessly cruel; towns must contain warmth, humor, trade, work, care, and ordinary routines.

### Core themes
- **The cost of the road:** Progress requires preparation, risk, and sometimes retreat.
- **Memory and evidence:** What people remember, what records preserve, and what places reveal may not agree.
- **Community under pressure:** A town is a network of people and needs, not a decorative menu.
- **The value of small choices:** A safer route, a recovered marker, a promise kept, or an item saved can shape what happens later.
- **The edge of the known:** The supernatural should complicate everyday life before it explains the universe.

### Avoid
Generic chosen-one certainty, constant grimness, lore dumps, random magic without rules, modern jokes that puncture the mood, and new currencies or systems that have no meaningful narrative or gameplay purpose. Supernatural spectacle should be rare enough to remain memorable.

## 3. Canon status rules

Use these labels when planning content:

- **Established:** Present in the game or agreed project documents. Preserve unless the project owner explicitly revises it.
- **Current interpretation:** A useful reading of existing material, not yet a final answer to a mystery.
- **Proposed:** A possible future direction. Do not present it as established lore in public-facing material.
- **Open:** Intentionally unanswered or requiring a decision.

Gameplay numbers, unlock order, interface labels, and placeholder descriptions are not automatically deep lore. A mechanic can express a theme without dictating a cosmology.

When two sources conflict, flag the conflict here rather than silently choosing whichever is newer. The project owner’s decision overrides this working document.

## 4. Established setting

### Ashmere
**Established:** Ashmere is the traveler’s first home base: a road-facing town with lanterns, services, work, trade, a forge, local people, and a gate to the Old Road. It should feel relatively safe and inhabited compared with the road beyond it.

Ashmere’s narrative function is to make returning matter. People remember work completed, supplies have sources and uses, services have distinct voices, and the player can see small changes in their own standing and capabilities.

### The Old Road
**Established:** The Old Road is the first major expedition space. It is a journey rather than a list of unrelated menus: named stops, finds, camp opportunities, hostile encounters, clues, route decisions, Road Pressure, and a return to town are part of the loop.

The South Fork presents two defining early approaches:
- **High Ridge:** Firmer, calmer ground. The route’s payoff is lower immediate pressure and the Ridgeway Cairn discovery.
- **Reed Cut:** A riskier lowland passage marked by cold blue light. The player can find a Bell Fragment, but the route carries more danger and leads to tracks beneath the blue light.

The road can feel watchful in narration. That atmosphere is established; the exact cause is **open**.

### Greyfen
**Established:** Greyfen is a working lowland marsh settlement with raised timber walks, stilted homes, low bridges, reed roofs, fishing platforms, peat stores, lantern docks, shallow dark water, and weathered stone footings. It is water-facing, materially different from Ashmere’s road-facing lantern-town identity.

Its first planned identity includes Reedgate landing, the Silted Cup, Float Market, the Drowned Anvil, Boundary Archive, a local Work Board, and the Marsh Path. Greyfen stories should emphasize water levels, routes, salvage, work, flood marks, and practical knowledge passed between residents.

The signature contract **A Dry Crossing** is established in the design: follow old posts for a safer, slower approach, or attempt a riskier reed-bed shortcut. The route marker and the changed route are story hooks, not just rewards.

### Shared geography and intended first-game scope
**Intended first-game geography:** Ashmere, the Old Road, Greyfen, Ironpass, Blackwood, and the Ruined March are all regions and locations intended to be explored in the first LEGEND game. They are not separate game installments. Ashmere and Greyfen have the current implementation foundations; Ironpass, Blackwood, and the Ruined March remain in-development and must not be described as playable until their hubs, stories, and tests exist. Future titles will revisit these same locations as the world changes, rather than treating each region as a separate sequel.

## 5. Established motifs and the early mystery: bells, lanterns, and the road

### Established elements
- The first major quest is titled **The Bell That Rang Once**.
- The traveler’s default memory includes waking beneath a dead lantern outside Ashmere.
- Bell marks and Bell Fragments recur in the road’s discoveries and crafting loop.
- Cold blue light appears in the Reed Cut; Lantern Wisps belong to the current enemy and event language.
- Route markers, old seals, ledgers, and records preserve traces of paths and events.
- Greyfen’s route has changed after the water rose, according to its early story hooks.

### Current interpretation
These elements support an early investigative thread about warnings, route safety, conflicting evidence, and possible human concealment. They may eventually intersect with the larger monster incursion or returning magic, but that connection is **not established**. They are not the explanation for the whole setting.

### Questions deliberately left open
1. What rang once, and who heard it?
2. Why did the traveler wake beneath a dead lantern?
3. Are the road’s strange signs supernatural, human-made, or a mixture of both?
4. What are Bell Fragments: pieces of an object, records of an event, keys, or something else?
5. Why does the blue light lure travelers, and what do its tracks mean?
6. What changed the Greyfen routes: weather, engineering, conflict, strange influence, or several causes?
7. Who understands the pattern, and what would it cost them to tell the truth?

Do not answer all of these in the first game. Each answer should create a sharper question, reframe an established scene, or reveal a meaningful human consequence.

## 6. People and continuity anchors

### Mara Vell
**Established:** Archive keeper, early guide, and source of lore. She helps connect discoveries to records and turns observations into journal or archive progress. Her knowledge should feel researched and partial, not omniscient.

### Old Brenn
**Established:** Ledger keeper who tracks proof and rewards. He represents the practical side of the road: records, goods, obligations, and the value of verifiable evidence.

### Captain Oric
**Current repository name:** Captain Oric, associated with road survival, the gate, and combat guidance in earlier design notes. Some older informal project notes have used the spelling “Olric.” **Continuity action:** confirm the intended spelling with the project owner before a lore-heavy release; do not create two characters or rename dialogue silently.

### Sella
**Established in the project systems:** A named Ashmere character with talk and errand progression. Keep her dialogue and role aligned with the current implementation before expanding her backstory.

### Other named roles
Innkeeper, Trader, blacksmith, and regional keepers are useful service roles. Their names, pasts, relationships, and private motives should be added to the canon ledger only when intentionally chosen. Do not let an asset prompt invent a permanent biography by accident.

### Character writing rules
- Give each recurring character a practical responsibility, an individual speech pattern, a want, and a boundary.
- Characters may disagree about events without one of them automatically being foolish or dishonest.
- Their knowledge comes from their jobs, histories, relationships, or firsthand evidence.
- Dialogue should reveal character while giving the player a useful action, emotional choice, rumor, or new perspective.
- A character’s portrait must match their established age, silhouette, clothing, materials, and role across assets and media.

## 7. World rules for writers and designers

1. **The mundane comes first.** Roads need maintenance; towns need food, trade, tools, records, and workers. Strange events disrupt existing lives.
2. **Evidence has provenance.** A clue should have a source and a reason it survived. A reward that also advances lore should say how the traveler found it.
3. **Mysteries have patterns.** Mystery is not randomness. Reuse motifs with variation and pay them off deliberately.
4. **Consequences travel.** Where feasible, a choice should echo in narration, available information, a relationship, a route, or the next place—not merely a one-time number.
5. **Retreat is a valid story beat.** The traveler can be prudent, frightened, injured, or outmatched without being framed as a failure.
6. **Regions have economies and silhouettes.** Greyfen must not read as Ashmere with a blue filter. Climate, materials, livelihood, routes, architecture, sound, and local concerns should differ.
7. **Keep supernatural rules legible, even when their meaning is hidden.** Players should be able to notice repeated conditions before they understand the cause.
8. **No lore by asset accident.** A generated symbol, background prop, or incidental character is not canon until reviewed.
9. **Mechanics support meaning.** Items need a source and use; currencies need distinct purpose; quest rewards should feed a useful next choice.
10. **Mystery is not a substitute for character.** People still have daily goals and relationships even if a larger mystery is underway.

## 8. Visual and audio canon

Use [ART-DIRECTION.md](ART-DIRECTION.md) as the technical visual authority. In brief:
- Grounded, weathered storybook dark fantasy.
- Charcoal, slate, moss green, weathered umber, parchment cream, tarnished gold.
- Warm amber lights for human shelter; cold-blue accents for uncanny phenomena.
- Readable silhouettes, one main focal point, clear foreground/midground/background, safe space for interface text.
- No text baked into runtime art, no watermarks, no accidental logos, no mismatched styles between character variants.

Audio should follow the same emotional map:
- **Ashmere:** low, restrained, human warmth; quiet work and shelter sounds.
- **Old Road:** wind, timber, distant wildlife, low unease, minimal melodic certainty.
- **Combat:** readable action cues and tension, not constant loudness.
- **Discovery:** brief, memorable motifs used sparingly; bells and lantern tones should become recognizable without explaining the mystery too early.
- **Greyfen:** water, wood, ropes, reeds, working docks and the acoustic space of a wetland settlement—not simply Ashmere music with added rain.

These are direction rules. A sound or visual cue becomes recurring canon only after its meaning is selected.

## 9. Continuity workflow

Before adding a major quest, character, place, faction, creature, or supernatural rule, record:

- **Name and spellings**
- **Status:** established / current interpretation / proposed / open
- **First appearance**
- **What people believe**
- **What is actually known**
- **Gameplay role**
- **Physical and audio motifs**
- **Promises made to the player**
- **Future questions or payoffs**
- **Affected existing scenes, saves, quests, assets, and tests**

After a change, update the relevant source file and this bible if it alters canon. A change to UI text or a balancing value alone usually does not require a lore edit.

## 10. Current canon decisions to make with the project owner

Keep these visible for intentional decisions rather than silently guessing:
- Confirm the canonical spelling and role for Captain Oric / “Olric.”
- Decide what the phrase “The Bell That Rang Once” literally refers to, and how soon the player learns that.
- Decide how much the first game resolves about the road versus leaving for later installments.
- Establish the traveler’s place in the central mystery without making every possible protagonist’s background identical.
- Approve each region’s local culture, important NPCs, and primary conflict before creating large amounts of dialogue or assets.

Until those decisions are made, create local stories that enrich the world while preserving uncertainty.

## 11. The canon test

New LEGEND content belongs if it could only happen in this world: practical medieval life meets a restrained, repeating mystery; choices matter; clues have provenance; places remember through people and objects; and the road tempts travelers to trade safety for answers.


## 12. Active story-arc proposal

The companion document [Mystery & Story Arc Blueprint](LEGEND_MYSTERY_AND_STORY_ARC.md) proposes a hidden explanation for the Bell, lanterns, route markers, and the Old Road's unreliable passages, then maps that explanation into a first-game arc and practical quest chains.

**Status remains proposed, not canon.** Its current working hypothesis is “the Bell is an alarm, not a summons.” Use it as a coherent development model while planning routes and quest callbacks, but do not present it as an established fact in player-facing dialogue until the project owner approves it. ## 13. Route-side stories and visible consequences

The v0.20.23 source adds **The Moved Cairn** as the first playable route-side story built around a persistent environmental decision. On the High Ridge, the player can restore a shifted cairn stone, record both its current and older alignment, or mark the hazard and leave it untouched. The choice persists in the existing save, can be turned in for a reward, and changes later dialogue and route narration.

**Verification status:** Source and regression test are committed; complete browser verification remains pending. This implementation is a pattern for future route stories, not proof of the larger Bell hypothesis.


## 14. Blackroot-to-Greyfen evidence bridge

The v0.20.24 source links the Blackroot seal to the Greyfen Boundary Stone only after the player has inspected the Blackroot mark. The second location shows the same older bell-mark beneath a newer cut angled toward the reed bed; inspecting it updates the quest log and lets Mara compare both surfaces in Greyfen’s Boundary Archive.

The finding is deliberately limited: it supports that the marker was altered, but does not prove who changed it or why. Mara then asks for a dated record or another physical trace to establish when the newer cut appeared.

**Verification status:** Implementation and regression coverage are committed; full browser verification remains pending.


## 15. Reedwatch field note and crossing-ledger lead

The v0.20.25 source adds the next main-story evidence beat after the player compares the Blackroot seal with the Greyfen boundary stone. On a later Old Road trip, the Reedwatch Shelter locker can yield a dated field note whose sketch already shows the newer cut two days after Greyfen logged the flood’s retreat. Mara confirms what the date supports—and what it cannot prove—then sends the player to Brenn's crossing ledger.

Brenn reports that two destinations were overwritten in a later hand and points toward an earlier weekly route return. The current story therefore establishes a narrow timeline and a record discrepancy, but does not yet identify the writer or decision-maker. Treat this as current implemented source direction; full gameplay verification remains pending.


## 16. The Earlier Weekly Return

The v0.20.26 source adds the earlier weekly route return to the Wayfarer's Camp discovery when Brenn's ledger discrepancy has been established. It records the lower crossing as **closed pending inspection**, then shows a later **open** correction with no initials. The wording distinguishes the record's contents from the unknown person who made the correction.

Brenn compares this return with the rewritten ledger; Mara then files the Blackroot, Greyfen boundary, Reedwatch, and weekly-return evidence together. The missing closure order becomes the next lead. Full integration and live-browser verification remain pending.


## 17. Legacy series-wide antagonist proposal — secondary / not approved

The companion [Series Myth Arc: The Pale Surveyor](LEGEND_SERIES_MYTH_ARC.md) and [Series & Title Reframe](LEGEND_SERIES_CONTINUITY_REFRAME.md) propose an antagonist whose goal is to make the world conform to one account, even when doing so causes people and routes to disappear into the Hollow Mile. Under this interpretation, the old route network began as a protective system; the Surveyor's decision to force agreement turned it into a means of suppressing contradictory routes, records, and eventually communities.

**Status:** Legacy proposal, superseded as the primary franchise mythology by the Monster Wars & Awakening direction. The Pale Surveyor, the Long Severance, and the Office of Measure are not approved central canon. Keep only as optional secondary lore if a later reconciliation finds a strong fit. The proposal is designed to build on—rather than replace—the current Bell/Old Road hypothesis: the Bell acts as a warning, while the repeated marks, newer cuts, altered ledgers, and missing closure order are early evidence of a larger pattern.

Use the series arc to coordinate future reveals. Keep local conflicts and ordinary human choices independent unless evidence links them; the Surveyor must not become the explanation for every monster, flood, crime, or hardship.


## 18. Legacy deep-canon proposal — retain for reference, not as the primary saga

The companion [Deep Canon Foundation](LEGEND_DEEP_CANON_FOUNDATION.md) expands the proposed Pale Surveyor arc into a shared framework for the road network's rules, the original historical failure, the modern institution, the long-range game structure, and continuity across player choices.

**Status:** Proposed, not locked canon. It introduces additional working names and events—including Aren Vey, Ivara, the Seven-Wagon Dispatch, and the Four Witnesses—that must not be presented as established facts until deliberately approved. Its most important design constraint is that the network influences routes and access through linked anchors and human compliance; it is not a limitless power that explains every supernatural event.

Keep at least half of local story conflicts independent of the central awakening, and do not make a character's full life secretly revolve around the Bell mystery.


## 20. The title and recurring geography

**Recommended working subtitle:** *LEGEND: The Unquiet Road*. This replaces the location-specific working title *Roads of Ashmere* in the visible title treatment so the first game is not framed as an Ashmere-only adventure. The intended first game spans every planned region; future releases are organized around new conflicts and consequences, and revisit the same geography.

See [Fantasy RPG Direction](LEGEND_FANTASY_RPG_DIRECTION.md) and [Game Reboot Plan](LEGEND_GAME_REBOOT_PLAN.md) for the current Monster Wars & Awakening premise and proposed Veil / Afterwake mythology. The title and geography correction does not make any proposed supernatural explanation established canon.


## 21. Reboot direction and superseded mythology

As of 2026-10-15, [Game Reboot Plan](LEGEND_GAME_REBOOT_PLAN.md) is the primary proposed direction for a broader dark-fantasy RPG. It reframes magic around the Veil and Five Arts, proposes the Last Mercy / Afterwake / Hollow Saint as a world-level story candidate, and treats Bells primarily as Ashmere currency and ordinary civic signaling rather than the central cosmology.

The older Pale Surveyor / Hollow Mile mythology in [Series Myth Arc](LEGEND_SERIES_MYTH_ARC.md) and [Deep Canon Foundation](LEGEND_DEEP_CANON_FOUNDATION.md) is **superseded as the default direction**. Preserve it as an archived proposal only. The subtitle and shared-geography corrections remain useful, but new story or gameplay content must not assume the old supernatural explanation is canon. The new Veil / Last Mercy lore is also proposed, not locked canon.
